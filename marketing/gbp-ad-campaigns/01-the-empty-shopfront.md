# Campaign 1 — "The Empty Shopfront"

**Format:** 8-slide still-image carousel (Midjourney only, no animation).

## 1. Research Summary

**Why this topic matters**
Irish high streets are full of businesses that invested heavily in their
physical presence — fit-out, signage, stock, staff — but never did the
equivalent work online. A shop can be beautiful and still be invisible,
because today the "high street" most customers walk down first is a Google
search results page and Google Maps. If the business isn't set up, verified,
photographed and optimised there, it functionally doesn't exist to a large
share of local searchers before they ever pass the physical window.

**Customer psychology**
Business owners are proud of their physical space — they can see it, touch
it, show it off. A Google Business Profile feels abstract and low-priority by
comparison, so it gets ignored ("I'll get to it eventually"). The Empty
Shopfront concept works because it makes the invisible *visible*: it takes a
feeling business owners already understand instinctively (a shop with no
sign would obviously fail) and maps it directly onto their neglected online
listing. It reframes GBP neglect from an abstract technical gap into a
concrete, visceral loss they can picture — the wasted rent on a shop nobody
walks into.

**Common business mistakes**
- Treating GBP as a "set it up once and forget it" listing rather than an
  active shop window that needs photos, posts, hours and offers kept current.
- Leaving the profile unclaimed or unverified, so competitors' listings (or
  outdated third-party data) rank instead.
- No photos, or only a handful of low-quality phone shots, so the listing
  looks abandoned next to competitors with a full gallery.
- Ignoring the "primary category" and services fields, so Google doesn't
  even know to show them for the searches that matter.

**Competitor advertising approaches**
Most local marketing agencies sell GBP management as a line item inside a
generic "digital marketing" bundle, advertised with dashboards, graphs and
stock-photo laptops. It reads as software, not as something a shop owner
should urgently care about. Nobody is making the physical-to-digital
metaphor visceral and premium — which leaves an open lane for a still-image
campaign that looks and feels like a luxury brand shoot rather than a
marketing agency ad.

**Why this concept will generate enquiries**
It doesn't ask the owner to understand SEO. It asks them to look at their
own shopfront and imagine it with no sign — an emotional gut-punch any owner
instantly understands — then resolves that fear across the carousel by
showing their business winning the exact same visibility online. The ad does
the persuading before the caption is even read.

## 2. Creative Brief

- **Advertisement title:** The Empty Shopfront
- **Main objective:** Drive Google Business Profile Management enquiries by
  making "invisible online" feel as unacceptable as "invisible on the
  street."
- **Target customer:** Owners of boutiques, cafés, salons and independent
  retail on Irish high streets who have a strong physical presence but a
  neglected or unclaimed GBP listing.
- **Emotional trigger:** Fear of wasted investment — "I built something
  beautiful and nobody can find it."
- **Pain point:** A great business that isn't converting passers-by into
  customers because it's invisible in local search.
- **Solution:** Suirview Digital's Google Business Profile Management —
  claiming, optimising, and actively maintaining the listing so it ranks and
  converts.
- **CTA:** "Get your free Google Business Profile check — see exactly what
  customers see before they walk in."

## 3. Visual Direction

- **Environment:** A real Irish market-town high street — sandstone or
  painted shopfronts, wet pavement catching soft light, the kind of street
  found in Kilkenny, Waterford or Cork. Golden hour, low sun, long shadows.
- **Camera angle:** Eye-level, straight-on approach framing throughout the
  set — as if a customer is walking toward the shop in each still.
- **Lighting:** Warm natural light, soft shadow falloff, practical shop
  lighting glowing from inside the window. No artificial studio look.
- **Composition:** Centred shopfront filling the middle third of frame,
  generous negative space above for a single line of type added in post,
  rule-of-thirds placement of the door.
- **Realism:** Photoreal, shot-on-a-real-camera quality — no illustration,
  no 3D render look.
- **Premium advertising style:** Muted, cinematic colour grade (think Aesop
  or an Apple Store campaign) — desaturated street tones against one warm
  accent (the shop's own signage colour, or a Suirview blue glow once the
  digital listing appears in later slides).
- **Branding placement:** Suirview Digital logo appears only as a small
  quiet mark bottom-right in the final slide, beside the Google Business
  Profile card overlay — never earlier, never large.
- **Consistency:** generate Slide 1 first, then reuse its `--seed` and pass
  it as `--sref` on Slides 2–8 so the street, light and shopfront read as
  the same continuous location throughout the carousel.

## 6. Self-Critique

| Metric | Score |
|---|---|
| Hook | 9/10 |
| Visual impact | 9/10 |
| Business owner appeal | 8/10 |
| Trust | 8/10 |
| Lead generation potential | 8/10 |

**Improvements applied:** Business owner appeal and trust were initially
7/10 — the original cut jumped straight from "empty shop" to "optimised
listing" without showing *why* it happens, which felt like a sales trick
rather than an honest diagnosis. Slide 4 ("Explain why it happens") was
added specifically to fix this: it names the real, common mistake (never
claiming/verifying the profile) so the owner recognises their own situation
rather than feeling accused. Lead generation potential was raised from 7 to
8 by making the CTA a low-friction "free check" rather than a sales pitch,
and by keeping the Suirview brand mark hidden until the final slide so the
carousel reads as insight first, pitch second.

## 11. Why This Should Generate Leads

It converts an abstract service (profile optimisation) into a fear the
owner already feels every day walking past their own shop — wasted
potential. Because the resolution unfolds across the carousel (shopfront
becomes the search result by Slide 6), the value proposition is understood
in seconds of swiping without reading a word of marketing copy, and the CTA
asks for almost nothing ("free check"), which is the lowest-friction way to
convert a business owner who is intrigued but hasn't yet decided to spend.
