# Campaign 2 — "The Google Maps Race"

## 1. Research Summary

**Why this topic matters**
For high-trust, high-stakes categories like dentists and medical clinics,
customers rarely scroll. They search "best dentist near me," glance at the
top 3 Local Pack results, and call one of them — usually within minutes.
Ranking position in the Local Pack is not a vanity metric here; it is
close to a direct multiplier on new patient enquiries. A clinic that is
excellent but ranks 6th effectively loses the race before the patient even
knows it exists.

**Customer psychology**
Patients searching for a dentist are anxious and want reassurance fast —
they default to whichever result looks most established, most reviewed,
and most "first." Being first isn't just visibility, it *reads* as
credibility ("if they're top of Google, they must be good"). This is why
ranking position and trust are psychologically fused for the searching
customer — and why business owners feel the sting of losing that race so
personally: it isn't just a lost click, it's a lost vote of confidence.

**Common business mistakes**
- Assuming clinical quality alone will "naturally" surface them in search.
- Incomplete or generic service categories/descriptions, so Google can't
  confidently match them to specific searches ("emergency dentist,"
  "teeth whitening near me").
- Few or no recent reviews, or reviews left unanswered, which both Google's
  algorithm and patients read as inactivity.
- Competitors actively posting updates, photos and Q&A responses while this
  clinic's profile sits static for months.

**Competitor advertising approaches**
Dental and medical marketing ads typically lean on clinical imagery (white
smiles, gleaming equipment) or price promotions. Almost none directly show
the *mechanism* of losing patients — the actual search race happening in
real time on a phone screen. That gap is the opportunity: showing the race
itself, side by side, is more persuasive than any before/after smile photo
because it's the exact moment revenue is won or lost.

**Why this concept will generate enquiries**
Clinic owners are commercially sophisticated and competitive by nature —
showing them, literally, a competitor's pin sitting above theirs on a map
triggers competitive urgency far more effectively than a generic "improve
your SEO" pitch. It's specific, measurable, and makes the stakes (a real
patient, a real phone call) unmistakably clear.

## 2. Creative Brief

- **Advertisement title:** The Google Maps Race
- **Main objective:** Generate GBP Management enquiries from dental and
  medical clinics by dramatising the real-time cost of poor Local Pack
  ranking.
- **Target customer:** Dentists, medical clinics, and other appointment-led
  local healthcare providers competing for "near me" searches.
- **Emotional trigger:** Competitive urgency / fear of losing to a rival
  practice.
- **Pain point:** Being outranked by a competitor for the exact searches
  that convert into new patients.
- **Solution:** Suirview Digital's GBP optimisation moves the clinic into
  the top 3 Local Pack positions that actually get called.
- **CTA:** "See where you rank right now — free Local Pack position
  check."

## 3. Visual Direction

- **Environment:** Two parallel worlds in one frame — a patient's hand
  holding a smartphone in a softly lit, realistic home or car interior, and
  a clean overhead/close-up of the Google Maps interface itself as the hero
  "environment."
- **Camera angle:** Macro/close-up on the phone screen for the search
  interaction, with a shallow-depth cutaway to the patient's face reacting
  (relief or disappointment) — mirrors real UGC-style phone footage but
  shot with commercial polish.
- **Lighting:** Neutral, bright, believable phone-glow lighting — no
  dramatic colour grading, this needs to feel like "this is literally
  happening on your phone right now."
- **Composition:** Vertical-first framing (9:16) — phone screen occupies
  70% of frame, patient's hand and soft background fills the rest.
- **Realism:** Exact, pixel-accurate recreation of the real Google Maps
  Local Pack UI — this must look like a genuine screen recording, not a
  stylised graphic.
- **Premium advertising style:** Clean UI typography, Google's real
  interface conventions respected, but graded with a subtle premium warmth
  so it doesn't look like a raw screenshot.
- **Branding placement:** Suirview Digital watermark appears only in the
  final "after" frame, small, bottom-right, beside the now-top-ranked
  listing.

## 5. Higgsfield Animation Prompt

- **Starting frame:** Close-up of the phone screen mid-search, cursor/text
  typing "best dentist near me" into the search bar.
- **Camera movement:** Static macro lock-off on the screen for the search
  and results reveal; subtle slow push-in as the Local Pack results populate
  top to bottom.
- **Transition effect:** A soft highlight/glow sweeps across the map pins —
  competitor pin glows first and lifts slightly with a "1st" badge; then a
  time-lapse style light flicker signals days passing; the client's pin
  then glows and rises past the competitor's, settling into the #1 slot
  with a satisfying subtle bounce.
- **Text animation:** Text 1 appears beside the competitor's rising pin:
  *"Your competitor is winning this search."* Text 2 appears once the
  client's pin overtakes: *"Not anymore."* Typography: clean geometric
  sans-serif, tight tracking, white with soft drop shadow for legibility
  over the map.
- **Motion style:** Snappy but controlled — quick enough to feel like a
  "race," but each beat holds for a full second so the ranking change reads
  clearly. No shaky handheld energy; this should feel precise, like a
  stock-ticker of visibility.
- **Duration:** 10 seconds (ideal for Reels/Shorts; can loop the pin-race
  beat for a 6-second bumper cut).

## 6. Full Storyboard (8 slides)

1. **Hook** — Phone screen, someone typing "best dentist near me." Text:
   *"Someone near you is searching right now."*
2. **Identify the problem** — Local Pack populates; competitor clinic sits
   #1, client clinic is missing from the top 3 or absent entirely. Text:
   *"Are you even in the running?"*
3. **Show consequences** — Cut to the patient tapping the competitor's
   "Call" button, a phone visibly ringing at the competitor's front desk.
   Text: *"That's a patient. Gone to someone else."*
4. **Explain why it happens** — Quick, clean graphic-style comparison of two
   GBP dashboards: competitor's profile shows recent reviews, weekly posts,
   full photo gallery; client's shows stale info, no recent activity. Text:
   *"Google rewards active, complete profiles."*
5. **Introduce GBP optimisation** — Suirview Digital team/laptop shot,
   updating the clinic's categories, services, and photos live. Text:
   *"We optimise every signal Google actually ranks on."*
6. **Show transformation** — Return to the map-pin race animation: client's
   pin rises and overtakes the competitor's, landing in position #1. Text:
   *"Now you're the first call."*
7. **Show business benefits** — Warm, human beat: receptionist answering a
   new-patient call, booking confirmation on a tablet screen. Text: *"More
   enquiries. More new patients. Every week."*
8. **Strong CTA** — Final clean frame of the clinic's listing sitting
   confidently at #1 with full stars and reviews visible, Suirview Digital
   watermark bottom-right. Text: *"See where you rank right now — free
   check."*

## 7. Self-Critique

| Metric | Score |
|---|---|
| Hook | 9/10 |
| Visual impact | 9/10 |
| Business owner appeal | 9/10 |
| Trust | 8/10 |
| Lead generation potential | 9/10 |

**Improvements applied:** Trust scored 7/10 initially because recreating a
real Google Maps UI risks looking like it implies a guaranteed #1 ranking,
which is not a promise any ethical agency should make. Fixed by rewording
slide 8's CTA from an implied guarantee ("We'll get you to #1") to an
honest, verifiable offer ("See where you rank right now — free check"),
and by keeping the visual metaphor (the "race") clearly presented as
illustrative competitive dynamics rather than a specific ranking promise.
This keeps the emotional punch while staying compliant and credible.

## 12. Why This Should Generate Leads

It replaces an abstract ranking metric with a literal, visible race the
owner can watch happen — which is both more persuasive and easier to grasp
than any SEO statistic. Because dentists and clinics are acutely aware of
local competitors by name, the "someone else is winning your patients"
framing creates immediate, personal urgency, and the free ranking check CTA
gives an easy, low-risk first step that converts curiosity into a
qualified enquiry.
