# Campaign 2 — "The Google Maps Race"

**Format:** 8-slide still-image carousel (Midjourney only, no animation).

## 1. Research Summary

**Why this topic matters**
For high-trust, high-stakes categories like dentists and medical clinics,
customers rarely scroll. They search "best dentist near me," glance at the
top 3 Local Pack results, and call one of them — usually within minutes.
Ranking position in the Local Pack is not a vanity metric here; it is
close to a direct multiplier on new patient enquiries. A clinic that is
excellent but ranks 6th effectively loses the race before the patient even
knows it exists.

**Customer psychology**
Patients searching for a dentist are anxious and want reassurance fast —
they default to whichever result looks most established, most reviewed,
and most "first." Being first isn't just visibility, it *reads* as
credibility ("if they're top of Google, they must be good"). This is why
ranking position and trust are psychologically fused for the searching
customer — and why business owners feel the sting of losing that race so
personally: it isn't just a lost click, it's a lost vote of confidence.

**Common business mistakes**
- Assuming clinical quality alone will "naturally" surface them in search.
- Incomplete or generic service categories/descriptions, so Google can't
  confidently match them to specific searches ("emergency dentist,"
  "teeth whitening near me").
- Few or no recent reviews, or reviews left unanswered, which both Google's
  algorithm and patients read as inactivity.
- Competitors actively posting updates, photos and Q&A responses while this
  clinic's profile sits static for months.

**Competitor advertising approaches**
Dental and medical marketing ads typically lean on clinical imagery (white
smiles, gleaming equipment) or price promotions. Almost none directly show
the *mechanism* of losing patients — the actual search race, frozen in the
moment it's decided. That gap is the opportunity: showing that decisive
moment, side by side, is more persuasive than any before/after smile photo
because it's the exact moment revenue is won or lost.

**Why this concept will generate enquiries**
Clinic owners are commercially sophisticated and competitive by nature —
showing them, literally, a competitor's pin sitting above theirs on a map
triggers competitive urgency far more effectively than a generic "improve
your SEO" pitch. It's specific, measurable, and makes the stakes (a real
patient, a real phone call) unmistakably clear.

## 2. Creative Brief

- **Advertisement title:** The Google Maps Race
- **Main objective:** Generate GBP Management enquiries from dental and
  medical clinics by dramatising the real cost of poor Local Pack ranking.
- **Target customer:** Dentists, medical clinics, and other appointment-led
  local healthcare providers competing for "near me" searches.
- **Emotional trigger:** Competitive urgency / fear of losing to a rival
  practice.
- **Pain point:** Being outranked by a competitor for the exact searches
  that convert into new patients.
- **Solution:** Suirview Digital's GBP optimisation moves the clinic into
  the top 3 Local Pack positions that actually get called.
- **CTA:** "See where you rank right now — free Local Pack position
  check."

## 3. Visual Direction

- **Environment:** Two parallel worlds across the set — a patient's hand
  holding a smartphone in a softly lit, realistic home or car interior, and
  close-up recreations of the Google Maps interface itself as the hero
  "environment" in several slides.
- **Camera angle:** Macro/close-up on the phone screen for the search
  interaction, with shallow-depth cutaways to the patient's face reacting
  (relief or disappointment) — mirrors real UGC-style phone photography but
  shot with commercial polish.
- **Lighting:** Neutral, bright, believable phone-glow lighting — no
  dramatic colour grading, this needs to feel like "this is literally
  happening on your phone right now."
- **Composition:** Vertical-first framing (4:5) — phone screen occupies
  70% of frame, patient's hand and soft background fills the rest.
- **Realism:** Exact, believable recreation of the real Google Maps Local
  Pack UI — this must look like a genuine screen capture, not a stylised
  graphic.
- **Premium advertising style:** Clean UI typography, Google's real
  interface conventions respected, but graded with a subtle premium warmth
  so it doesn't look like a raw screenshot.
- **Branding placement:** Suirview Digital watermark appears only in the
  final "after" slide, small, bottom-right, beside the now-top-ranked
  listing.
- **Consistency:** generate Slide 1 first and reuse its `--seed` across
  Slides 2–8 so the phone, hand and UI style stay visually matched
  throughout the carousel.

## 6. Self-Critique

| Metric | Score |
|---|---|
| Hook | 9/10 |
| Visual impact | 9/10 |
| Business owner appeal | 9/10 |
| Trust | 8/10 |
| Lead generation potential | 9/10 |

**Improvements applied:** Trust scored 7/10 initially because recreating a
real Google Maps UI risks looking like it implies a guaranteed #1 ranking,
which is not a promise any ethical agency should make. Fixed by wording
Slide 8's CTA as an honest, verifiable offer ("See where you rank right
now — free check") rather than an implied guarantee ("We'll get you to
#1"), and by keeping the visual metaphor (the "race") clearly presented as
illustrative competitive dynamics rather than a specific ranking promise.
This keeps the emotional punch while staying compliant and credible.

## 11. Why This Should Generate Leads

It replaces an abstract ranking metric with a literal, visible comparison
the owner can study slide by slide — which is both more persuasive and
easier to grasp than any SEO statistic. Because dentists and clinics are
acutely aware of local competitors by name, the "someone else is winning
your patients" framing creates immediate, personal urgency, and the free
ranking check CTA gives an easy, low-risk first step that converts
curiosity into a qualified enquiry.
