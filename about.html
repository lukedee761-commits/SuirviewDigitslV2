02 — Before this

I was building things long before I built websites.

I didn’t come to this from a design degree. I came to it from a building site — and honestly, that’s the part of my background I lean on most.

At sixteen

A carpentry apprenticeship

I started work in construction at sixteen, apprenticed to carpentry. It’s where I learned the thing I still work by: measure it properly the first time, or you’ll be doing the whole job twice.

After that

Builder’s labourer

A tough stretch ended the apprenticeship, and I went labouring instead — pouring concrete, fitting insulation, whatever the day in front of me needed. Site work doesn’t much care how you’re feeling. You turn up, and the job gets finished.

September 2025

Back to study, to become an engineer

I decided I wanted to be an engineer, so I went and did it properly: a QQI Level 5 in Mechanical Engineering and Technology. Drawings, tolerances, and the discipline of designing something on paper before you commit to building it. That habit is exactly how I approach a website now.

03 — Why it exists

It started with two broken feet.

There’s no story here about spotting a gap in the market. I got injured, I couldn’t find work that suited me, so I built something of my own instead.

JAN 2026

I broke both feet

Both of them, at once, halfway through the engineering course. Every kind of work I’d ever done was work you do standing up. I went looking for a job that suited what I could actually manage — and there wasn’t one.

FEB 2026

I stopped looking, and started building

About a month after the injury, I stopped waiting for someone to hire me and started SuirViewDigital. It was work I could do sitting down, and work I could get properly good at.

SINCE

I finished the course anyway

I came out the other side of it with the QQI Level 5, and kept building sites at the same time. Same approach as the site work, in the end: you turn up, and you finish the job.

04 — How I work

You see the website before you spend anything.

You see it before you pay

I build your site first — a real one, live on a real address, with your actual business in it. Not a mockup, and not a template with your logo dropped on top. If you like it, you pay and I finish it with every change you want. If you don’t, you owe me nothing.

You deal with me

No account manager, no project coordinator, no ticket queue. The person you brief is the person designing it, building it, and answering the phone when you ring.

Available 24/7

Not a support window — me. A small business doesn’t only run into problems between nine and five, and I’d rather you could reach me when it actually happens.

Premium work, at a price a small business can actually pay

That trade-off is deliberate. I’d rather build something genuinely good and charge what a local business can afford than charge agency rates for something ordinary. It’s a slower way to grow, and it’s the way I want to grow.

Any business, tailored to it

I haven’t found a trade I won’t take on. Tell me what you do and how you actually work, and I’ll build for that — rather than bending you to fit a template.

Trust is the whole foundation

I’m building this from nothing, which means every site I put out has to be good enough that you’d mention me to someone else. That isn’t a slogan — for a one-person studio it’s the only way this works.

05 — Contact
