05 — Search & AI visibility

Findability

The fifth layer · 6 min read

In short: The site is real. Nobody ever arrives at it.

This is the layer that decides whether any of the others get seen. And it is the layer that changed most in the last two years.

Search is answering, not just listing

In July 2025 the Pew Research Center published the most solid public data yet on what AI summaries do to clicks. They tracked the actual browsing of 900 US adults through March 2025 — 68,879 unique Google searches, of which 12,593 produced an AI summary.

Users who met an AI summary clicked a result on 8% of visits. Users who did not clicked on 15% — nearly twice as often. And the links inside the AI summary itself barely got touched: just 1% of visits to a page with a summary produced a click on one of its citations.

People were also more likely to simply stop. The search answered them, and they were done. Around one in five Google searches in that period produced a summary.

What this means if you are a local business

It does not mean search is finished. It means the terms have shifted. If an assistant is going to answer who is a good electrician near Thurles in one paragraph, the question becomes whether your business is in the material that answer is built from.

That is not a different discipline from search engine optimisation — it is the same discipline with the stakes moved. Clear, factual, well-structured pages that state plainly what you do and where you do it are what both a search engine and a language model can use. Vague marketing prose is what neither can.

  • A page per service and a page per area you actually cover, named plainly
  • Business name, address, phone and hours marked up so machines can read them
  • A Google Business Profile that matches the site exactly
  • Real, specific answers to the questions customers ask
  • Reviews, which people and summaries both lean on heavily
What it costs you

The website is real, the work is good, and nobody ever arrives. This is the most expensive layer to skip, because the cost is entirely invisible — you cannot miss the customers who never found you.

The honest limitation

The Pew study is US adults, tracked over one month, on Google specifically. It does not tell you what happens to an Irish tradesman's site, and anyone quoting it as though it does is overreaching. What it does establish, on real browsing data rather than a survey, is the direction and the rough size of the shift — and that is enough to plan against.
