06 — Performance & security

The foundation

The sixth layer · 5 min read

In short: It works for a year. Then one morning it quietly doesn't.

Speed, hosting, backups, security, updates. This is the layer with no visible output, which is exactly why it is the first thing cut from a cheap quote.

A tenth of a second

In 2019, Google commissioned 55 and Deloitte to isolate site speed as a variable and measure what it did to behaviour. They studied 37 leading European and American brand sites, collecting data on over 30 million user sessions, monitored hour by hour for 30 days.

They then looked at what a 0.1 second improvement in load speed was worth.

The lead generation figures are the relevant ones for a service business. A 0.1 second improvement was associated with a 5.5% increase in progression from homepage to the first step of a form, and a 21.6% increase in progression from that first step to actually submitting it.

A tenth of a second is not perceptible. It is not something a visitor could tell you about afterwards. It still moved the number.

The half that is not speed

The rest of this layer is the unglamorous maintenance that decides whether a site is still working in two years:

  • A current SSL certificate, and something watching for its expiry
  • Software and plugins kept updated, because outdated ones are how sites get defaced
  • Backups that have actually been tested by restoring one
  • Hosting with someone answerable when it goes down
  • Someone whose job it is to notice — before a customer does
What it costs you

It works for a year, then one morning it quietly doesn't, and you find out from a customer who could not book. A site that is offline is worse than no site, because people were expecting to find it.

The honest limitation

The Deloitte study covered large retail, travel, luxury and lead generation brands, not small local businesses, and correlation over 30 million sessions is not proof that speed caused every gain. The scale is what makes it worth citing: the effect was consistent enough, across enough sessions and enough sectors, to be taken seriously as a direction.

This is the layer you are paying for when a website is maintained rather than just built. It is the difference between a website and a website that is still working next year.
